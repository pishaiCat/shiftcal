    Questions

    Why do I need an “anchor date” instead of a start date?

    A start date is easy to misremember. An anchor is a single date you are 100% sure about — for example, “on Tuesday 14th I definitely worked a Night, and that was day 3 of my pattern”. From that one certain fact, the repeating sequence fixes every other date, forwards and backwards, forever.

    How are night shifts counted?

    A night shift belongs to the date it starts on. If you start at 19:00 on Tuesday and finish at 07:00 on Wednesday, that is Tuesday’s shift in the calendar, the counts, the lookup and the .ics export.

    Will daylight saving time break my calendar?

    No. Dates here are calculated as calendar days, not as 24-hour chunks of time, so the 23-hour spring day and the 25-hour autumn day cannot skip or double a date. Your shift times stay as wall-clock times; only the real length of that one night changes (11h in spring, 13h in autumn).

    What does the .ics export do?

    It creates one calendar event per working shift for the months shown, with the correct timezone so events land at the right wall-clock time across daylight-saving changes. Import it into Google Calendar, Apple Calendar or Outlook. Days off are not exported.

    Is my data uploaded anywhere?

    No. There is no server and no account. Your pattern lives in your browser and in any share link you choose to copy.
