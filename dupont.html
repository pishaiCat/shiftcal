DuPont Shift Calendar — Free 28-Day Rotation Calendar

The DuPont schedule is a 28-day rotating 12-hour pattern famous for one thing: a full 7 consecutive days off in every cycle. Four crews rotate so the plant is covered 24/7; each crew works the same sequence, staggered by a week. Each crew works 14 of the 28 days.

How the DuPont pattern works

  • Days 1–4: 4 Night shifts
  • Days 5–7: 3 days off
  • Days 8–10: 3 Day shifts
  • Day 11: 1 day off (the “turnaround” day)
  • Days 12–14: 3 Night shifts
  • Days 15–17: 3 days off
  • Days 18–21: 4 Day shifts
  • Days 22–28: 7 days off — the famous week-long break
NNNNOOODDDONNNOOODDDDOOOOOOO

In the shorthand used by schedulers it reads: 4N → 3 off → 3D → 1 off → 3N → 3 off → 4D → 7 off (4+3+3+1+3+3+4+7 = 28). One cycle includes a 72-hour work week, which “pays for” the 7-day break. Patterns vary by employer — edit times and types to match yours.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do I work per DuPont cycle?

14 of 28 days: 4 nights, 3 days, 3 nights and 4 days, with 14 days off including the 7-day break.

Why is week 2 the hardest week?

Week 2 packs 6 twelve-hour shifts (3 days + 3 nights) — 72 hours — separated only by the single turnaround day. That heavy week is what funds the 7-day break.

How do I set my anchor date?

Pick one date you are 100% certain about — e.g. “on 3 May I worked a Night and it was day 2 of the cycle” — and enter that in the generator. Everything else follows.

Do night shifts count on the start date?

Always. A night starting Tuesday evening is Tuesday’s shift in the calendar, the counts, the lookup and the .ics file, even though it ends Wednesday morning.

Is this tool an employer scheduling system?

No. It is a worker-side calendar only: no account, no manager, no workplace data. Always check against your official roster.
